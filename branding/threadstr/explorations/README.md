# Threadstr v1 exploration archive

This archive records the iterations behind the first Threadstr logo. It contains
18 retained concept boards across five documented rounds plus three additional
studies. These are exploratory raster images, not interchangeable final assets.
Use the approved package in [../v1](../v1/) for delivery.

[Browse all 18 boards visually](index.html). The PNG boards are byte-for-byte
copies of their saved originals. Saved prompt Markdown has formatting normalized
by the repository formatter; the design instructions are preserved.

## Brief and naming

The product is a private personal project notebook: notes, decisions, meeting
records, and progress form chat-like project threads. The initial naming study
compared **trackstr** and **threadstr**, combining tracking/threading with the
folklore idea of a trickster. The user chose **threadstr**, preferring its spoken
rhythm to the `ck + str` transition in trackstr.

The visual brief began with simplicity, flat design, minimalism, and a text-first
logo. The existing app's bold system-sans typography and neutral light/dark
surfaces informed the work. Later requests introduced larger changes across
multiple letters, conversation imagery, mythological character, and youthful
irregularity. The concept boards were generated with the built-in image tool;
no named commercial typeface was established as the final logo font.

## 4. Threadstr-only refinement — J to L

The user selected the name threadstr and asked for D's flow and H's playfulness,
while noting that tangled paths could contradict the app's promise of clarity.

| Board                                          | Idea                                        | What it explored                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| [J — Easy Flow](J-threadstr-easy-flow.png)     | Open connections through the middle letters | Smooth motion without a full weave               |
| [K — Quick Reply](K-threadstr-quick-reply.png) | The ending rises like an interjection       | Conversational rhythm and small speech-tail cuts |
| [L — Offbeat](L-threadstr-offbeat.png)         | Controlled tilts and varied proportions     | A more spontaneous, independent character        |

K was refined from an initial Inside Joke draft to clarify its a and distinguish
its composition. [Generation and refinement prompts](prompts-round-4.md).

## 5. Ribbon-density study — M to O

The user returned to D's continuous ribbons and asked for three density levels,
explicitly treating D itself as the dense endpoint. This was a deliberate
comparison of complexity within one family, following the earlier concern about
tangles.

| Board                                           | Requested density     | Construction                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| [M — Simple / a](M-threadstr-ribbon-simple.png) | Low                   | One flowing e–a connection                                |
| [N — Medium / b](N-threadstr-ribbon-medium.png) | Medium                | A contained e–a–d weave with clear letters on either side |
| [O — Dense / c](O-threadstr-ribbon-dense.png)   | High, comparable to D | Ribbon connections across h–r–e–a–d–s                     |

N was refined to remove the extra h–r sweep and make the middle density more
distinct. [Generation and refinement prompts](prompts-round-5.md).

## Selection and handoff

The user selected **c / O — Dense**, described as the “thin version,” for v1,
and requested full wordmarks plus an abbreviated **thr** social mark in light
and dark forms. The selected concept board is the authoritative visual
reference. Export details and the interpretation of thinness are recorded in
the [v1 package](../v1/README.md).

Earlier assistant preferences were design suggestions, not user approvals.
Only the final c/O selection is the approved v1 direction. This archive records
the many attempts without replacing historical product-title mentions or
integrating a rebrand into the app.
